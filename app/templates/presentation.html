SLIDE 05 • MATHEMATICAL METHODOLOGY

Distance Metrics: Euclidean (L2) vs. Manhattan (L1)

Robust Centroid Formulations Under Volatile Airfare Outliers

Euclidean Distance (\(L_2\) Norm)
Standard K-Means formulation: \(d_2(x, \mu) = \sqrt{\sum (x_i - \mu_i)^2}\)
Squared Error Penalty: Squaring large errors gives disproportionate leverage to rare, extreme fare spikes (e.g. ₹95,000 emergency tickets).
Centroid Pull: Outliers artificially shift cluster centroids away from representative corridor patterns.
Validation Result: Yields Silhouette score of 0.4369 at K=4.
Manhattan Distance (\(L_1\) Norm) ★ Superior Formulation
K-Medians formulation: \(d_1(x, c) = \sum |x_i - c_i|\)
Linear Residual Growth: Evaluates absolute differences, maintaining equal penalty per unit deviation.
Median Robustness: Component-wise medians resist price spikes, producing tighter and more interpretable clusters.
Empirical Superiority: Achieves Silhouette score of 0.4858 at K=4 (+11.2% higher cluster cohesion).

SLIDE 09 • LIMITATIONS & SAMPLING BIASES

Limitations, Sampling Biases & Academic Rigor

Critical Evaluation of Observational Boundaries and Data Censoring

1. Observation Horizon Truncation
Fares are recorded up to 49 days prior, omitting ultra-early bird inventory released 6–12 months ahead.
Impact: Focuses analysis specifically on the final 7-week yield management curve.
2. Survivorship & Censoring Bias
Aggregator scrapes only capture unsold inventory; fully booked flights disappear from view.
Impact: Departure-week fares are biased toward either expensive unsold premium seats or distressed inventory.
3. Metropolitan Hub Concentration
Dataset concentrates on primary Indian Tier-1 hubs (DEL, BOM, BLR).
Impact: Monopolistic regional routes may exhibit different elasticity patterns.
4. Exogenous Shock Omission
Macroeconomic shocks (aviation fuel ATF prices, weather disruptions) are unobserved.
Defense: Corridor-level aggregation dampens single-ticket noise and isolates structural trends.
