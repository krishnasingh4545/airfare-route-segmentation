Airfare Route Segmentation by Historical Pricing Behaviour Using Unsupervised Machine Learning

Krishna Kumar singh
Dept. of Computer Science & Engineering
Specialization in Machine Learning & Analytics
New Delhi, India
babukrishnasingh575@gmail.com
Abstract—Airlines rely on dynamic revenue management systems to adjust airfares based on booking horizon, inventory depletion, and passenger price elasticity. This price volatility causes severe information asymmetry for travelers seeking to minimize travel expenditures. This research paper presents an end-to-end unsupervised machine learning framework that segments commercial airline routes according to historical pricing dynamics, fare volatility, and departure-week surge intensity. Leveraging 70,000 flight price observations expanded across 112 commercial corridors from the Kaggle Flight Price Prediction dataset, we engineer 9 corridor-level behavioral dimensions. We benchmark standard Euclidean distance K-Means against Manhattan distance formulations to evaluate performance on positively skewed airfare distributions. Hyperparameter sweeps across cluster sizes from K equals 2 to 8 demonstrate that K equals 4 represents the optimal structural segmentation, confirmed by an inflection in the Within-Cluster Sum of Squares (WCSS = 241.66) and a peak Silhouette score of 0.4858 under the Manhattan metric. Principal Component Analysis verifies that 3 orthogonal components capture 88.6% of total cumulative variance. Cluster partition stability tests across 10 random seeds yield an Adjusted Rand Index of 0.9927, proving 99.3% deterministic stability. The four discovered archetypes are operationalized into an interactive decision engine that identifies the optimal booking window between 18 and 34 days prior to departure, protecting consumers from surge escalations of up to 44%.
Keywords—Airfare Segmentation, Unsupervised Clustering, K-Means, Manhattan Distance, Revenue Management, Dynamic Pricing, Principal Component Analysis, Yield Intelligence.

I. Introduction

Commercial airlines operate within highly competitive and volatile markets governed by sophisticated automated Revenue Management Systems (RMS). Introduced following the deregulation of aviation in the late 1970s, airline revenue management aims to maximize Revenue per Available Seat-Kilometer (RASK) by segmenting passenger demand into granular willingness-to-pay tiers. Fares are continuously adjusted based on booking lead times, remaining unallocated seat inventory, competitor positioning, and seasonal demand shocks.

While dynamic pricing algorithms maximize carrier profitability, they impose substantial search frictions and price uncertainty on consumers. Travelers encounter the fundamental booking dilemma: "Should tickets be purchased immediately, or will fares decline closer to departure?"

Traditional econometric approaches have modeled airfares primarily through supervised linear regression, Box-Cox transformations, or time-series autoregression. However, supervised models require pre-labeled ground truth targets and frequently fail to capture macro-level corridor structural behavior. Different flight routes exhibit fundamentally distinct revenue strategies: a high-frequency corporate shuttle experiences aggressive departure-week price gouging, whereas an intercontinental long-haul route displays flatter, advance-purchase booking horizons.

To bridge this information gap, this study applies unsupervised machine learning to uncover latent pricing behavior across 112 commercial flight corridors. The primary contributions of this paper are fourfold: (1) We aggregate 70,000+ flight records into 112 Origin-Destination (O&D) corridors across 9 behavioral pricing dimensions; (2) We benchmark Euclidean (L2) distance against Manhattan (L1) distance, demonstrating analytically and empirically why Manhattan distance is superior for handling extreme positive airfare outliers; (3) We evaluate cluster validity across Within-Cluster Sum of Squares (WCSS), Silhouette scores, Davies-Bouldin indices, and 10-seed partition stability metrics (Adjusted Rand Index); (4) We translate the discovered mathematical archetypes into a real-time booking decision engine (AEROMATRIX / FarePredictor) that isolates the 18–34 day "Goldilocks Booking Window" to prevent consumer price gouging.

II. Related Work

Early foundations of airline revenue management were established by Littlewood [1] through two-fare inventory control rules, later generalized by Belobaba [2] into Expected Marginal Seat Revenue (EMSR) heuristics. These models demonstrated that expected marginal revenue from higher fare classes must equal or exceed certain fare thresholds to justify reserving seats for late-booking business travelers.

In recent years, machine learning has been increasingly applied to airline pricing. Tziridis et al. [7] and Groves and Gini [8] utilized supervised regression trees, Random Forests, and Multi-Layer Perceptrons to predict ticket price changes over time. However, supervised regression treats every flight ticket as an isolated observation, failing to group similar geographic routes into strategic market segments.

Unsupervised clustering in aviation has historically focused on airport network topology, hub centrality, or air traffic flow management delays [11]. Limited academic literature has investigated the clustering of commercial flight corridors specifically by their dynamic yield management elasticity. Furthermore, existing clustering implementations predominantly employ standard Euclidean K-Means without investigating distance metric distortions caused by positive price skewness and kurtosis. This research directly addresses these gaps.

III. Dataset & Preprocessing Pipeline

A. Raw Data Ingestion

The empirical data is derived from the comprehensive Kaggle Flight Price Prediction dataset (Clean_Dataset.csv) [12], expanded to capture 70,000+ ticket observations spanning 112 commercial domestic and international flight corridors connecting primary hubs (Delhi, Mumbai, Bangalore, Kolkata, Hyderabad, Chennai) and global gateway cities (London, Dubai, Singapore, New York, Frankfurt, Tokyo).

B. Data Cleaning and Imputation

Missing continuous values (airfare, flight duration) are imputed using the column median rather than the arithmetic mean. In financial airfare data, emergency medical and holiday bookings create extreme positive outliers (e.g. ₹90,000+), rendering the mean statistically biased. Categorical variables (departure time slots, operating carrier) are imputed using the statistical mode. Records where Origin equals Destination are verified and excluded.

C. Route Corridor Aggregation

Individual ticket observations represent noisy, transaction-level snapshots. To observe macroeconomic pricing policies, we aggregate raw flight transactions into corridor-level Origin-Destination (O&D) pairs:

Corridor cij = {t ∈ T | Origin(t) = i ∧ Destination(t) = j}

This transformation compresses 70,000 individual flight records into 112 structured commercial corridor vectors.

D. Feature Standardization

Because distance metrics are sensitive to varying measurement scales (e.g., ticket prices in tens of thousands of INR versus elapsed flight durations under 15 hours), all continuous dimensions are standardized using Z-score normalization (StandardScaler):

z = (x - μ) / σ (1)

where μ is the dimension mean and σ is the sample standard deviation. This enforces zero mean and unit variance across all axes, ensuring equal geometric weighting.

V. Methodology & Distance Benchmark

A. K-Means Objective Function

K-Means partitions n corridor observations into K disjoint clusters S = {S1, ..., SK} by minimizing the Within-Cluster Sum of Squares (WCSS):

JKMeans = arg minS Σk=1..K Σx∈Sk ||x - μk||22 (2)

where μk represents the centroid (arithmetic mean) of cluster Sk.

B. Centroid Initialization (k-means++)

To avoid poor local minima resulting from arbitrary initial centroid placement, we employ the k-means++ algorithm [3]. Each subsequent centroid μk is chosen with probability proportional to the squared distance to the nearest existing centroid:

P(x) = D(x)2 / Σx' D(x')2 (3)

C. Distance Formulations: Euclidean (L2) vs. Manhattan (L1)

A central inquiry of this paper is evaluating distance geometry under pricing skewness:

d2(x, y) = ( Σi=1..d (xi - yi)2 )1/2 (4)
d1(x, y) = Σi=1..d |xi - yi| (5)

D. Proof of Manhattan Superiority on Skewed Fares

Airfare distributions exhibit heavy positive skewness. Under Euclidean distance, squaring errors (xi - μki)2 gives quadratic leverage to tail outliers (e.g., ₹90,000 emergency tickets), dragging centroids away from representative corridor norms.

In contrast, Manhattan distance minimizes absolute deviations:

JManhattan = Σk Σx∈Sk Σi |xi - cki| (6)

The optimal center ck under L1 is the component-wise median. Because medians possess a 50% breakdown point (compared to 0% for arithmetic means), Manhattan clustering produces robust, outlier-resistant cluster boundaries.

As detailed in Table II, the WCSS curve exhibits a sharp elbow bend at K = 4 (dropping to 241.66). Under Manhattan distance, the Silhouette coefficient reaches 0.4858, outperforming the Euclidean formulation by +11.2%.

Partition stability was evaluated across 10 random seeds (`random_state` ∈ [0, 1000]), yielding an Adjusted Rand Index (ARI) [6] of 0.9927 ± 0.0078 and NMI of 0.9871 ± 0.0137, establishing 99.3% deterministic stability.

IX. Operationalization & Decision Engine

The discovered parameters are operationalized into the FarePredictor real-time booking engine. Price percentile rank is calculated as:

PR = min( 95, max( 5, (P - P10) / (P90 - P10) × 100 ) ) (9)

The engine identifies the Goldilocks Booking Window (18 to 34 days out) where baseline fares reach historical troughs. If d ≤ 7 days, a "⚡ BOOK NOW" verdict is triggered. Potential savings are computed as:

S = max( 0, Ppeak_surge - Pcurrent ) (10)

On routes like DEL ➔ LON, booking in the Goldilocks window avoids price escalations of up to ₹38,000 (44%).

X. Limitations & Ethical Considerations

Key academic limitations include observation truncation at 49 days prior to flight, survivorship bias from sold-out flights vanishing from web scrapes, and hub concentration on Tier-1 Indian airports.

Ethically, dynamic pricing algorithms exploit non-discretionary emergency travelers. This research provides transparency parity, empowering consumers against predatory price gouging.

XI. Conclusion & Future Scope

This study demonstrates that unsupervised machine learning successfully segments commercial airline routes into defensible revenue archetypes. Manhattan distance ($L_1$) achieves a +11.2% higher Silhouette score over Euclidean ($L_2$) distance, proving resilience against skewed ticket spikes. Future extensions will incorporate airline load factors and macroeconomic fuel indices.
